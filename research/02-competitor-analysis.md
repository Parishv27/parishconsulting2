# Phase 2: Competitive Niche Analysis
**Niche:** K-12 school improvement, instructional leadership, and principal coaching consultancies
**Buyer:** Superintendents, assistant superintendents, directors of federal programs, building principals
**Researched:** August 16, 2026
**Method:** Firecrawl web search for candidate discovery, then direct page scraping of the top five

## A. Candidate Pool (10 firms evaluated)

Parish competes in two overlapping arenas. The first is the **national brand tier**, firms with
research libraries and multi-state contracts. The second is the **boutique founder-led tier**,
single-expert LLCs that win on relationship, proximity, and credibility as a former practitioner.
Parish sits squarely in the second tier but prices toward the first, so both were scored.

## C. Competitor Profiles

### 1. New Leaders (newleaders.org) | Score 74/80

**Headline:** "New Leaders Is Redefining How Schools Are Led"
**Primary CTAs:** "Explore Our Programs & Services," "Share With A Future Principal," "Discover The National Aspiring Principals Fellowship"
**Value proposition:** Develops school leaders who "remove barriers to success for underestimated and underserved students," backed by claimed possession of "the strongest evidence base of any education leadership program in the country."

**Design:** Navy primary with orange accents on white. Clean sans-serif throughout. Authentic
documentary-style photography of real educators and students, not stock. Hero image with text
overlay, modular card sections, hexagonal decorative motifs.

**Services:** National Aspiring Principals Fellowship, Leadership Development Pathways, Principal
Coaching, Instructional Leadership Team Summits, Leading and Learning Workshops, Strategic District
Consulting, Aspiring Teachers Accelerator.

**Social proof:** Named alumni spotlights with cohort years, direct alumni quotes, a dedicated
Impact and Advocacy section in primary navigation.

**Strengths.** The evidence-base claim is the single strongest positioning line in the category.
Impact is elevated to a top-level nav item rather than buried. Equity language is specific
("underestimated and underserved") instead of decorative.
**Weaknesses.** Nonprofit scale makes the site feel institutional rather than personal. There is no
single human face to hire. Slow under image load.
**What Parish should take.** Give impact its own top-level nav entry. Use precise equity language.
**What Parish should not copy.** The facelessness. Parish's advantage is that a district hires a
specific, named, credentialed person.

### 2. K12 Coalition (k12coalition.com) | Score 67/80

**Headline:** "Helping Schools Do The Work That Matters"
**Primary CTAs:** "Connect with Us," "Let's Connect"
**Value proposition:** Opens on a threat statistic. "70% of school initiatives fail to achieve their
desired outcomes." Positions itself as the research-backed antidote, staffed by people who held
district and state-level roles.

**Design:** Professional blues and whites. Clean modern sans-serif. Modular card-based layout with
alternating text and image blocks and generous whitespace.

**Services:** Executive Coaching, Instructional Framework Design, District and School Improvement
Planning, Assessment and Professional Development Strategies, Accelerated Learning Planning.

**Social proof:** Named principal testimonial (Nehemiah Thomas) praising personalized executive
coaching. Named case study: Promise Academy, "double-digit literacy and math gains." Seven named
consultants with real titles.

**Strengths.** Best conversion architecture of the five. The problem statistic in the first
viewport is the most effective opening device in the category. Every consultant is named and titled,
which converts an abstract firm into hireable humans.
**Weaknesses.** Consulting is buried under a "District Services" nav item beneath four commercial
product lines. Visual identity is interchangeable with any edtech vendor.
**What Parish should take.** Lead with the cost of inaction, then the named-outcome case study.
Steal the structure of the Promise Academy proof block.
**What Parish should not copy.** Burying the core offer under product navigation.

**Subheadline:** "A System-Level Approach to Teaching, Learning, and Leadership"
**Supporting line:** "K-12 education does not operate in silos, and neither should professional development."
**Primary CTA:** "Contact Us"
**Value proposition:** Partners across the entire K-12 continuum to ensure alignment across grade

**Design:** Blue-toned branding, sans-serif type, professional classroom and educator photography,

**Services:** Fourteen named topic areas including Alternative Education, Attendance, Collaborative

### 4. Zeal Education Group (zeal-ed.com) | Score 60/80

**Headline:** "Outside View. Inside Perspective."
**Positioning line:** "Not career consultants. 100+ superintendents, principals, and thought leaders solving your district's most vexing challenges."
**Value proposition:** Every consultant is a former practitioner, not a career consultant. Services
include school turnaround support and executive search for districts.

**Design:** Modern, dense expert-directory layout. Each expert card carries a photo, doctoral
credential, and a row of pill-shaped expertise tags (Systemwide Improvement, School Turnaround,
Principal Supervision and Coaching, Equity-Driven Leadership, Curriculum and Instruction, and so on).

**Services:** School Turnaround Support, Systemwide Improvement, Principal Supervision and Coaching,
Curriculum and Instruction, Board Governance, Superintendent and Cabinet Searches, Strategic Planning,
Family and Community Engagement, Multilingual Learner Programs.

**Social proof:** The expert bench itself is the proof. Bios cite hard numbers: "sustained graduation
rates above 98 percent," "revising the leader evaluation model for Providence Public Schools,"
"directing a K-12 curriculum audit for Hartford Public Schools." Note: the live site blocked direct
scraping, so this profile is built from indexed page content rather than a full page render.

**Strengths.** "Not career consultants" is the sharpest differentiator statement in the whole niche
and it is precisely the claim Parish can make more credibly than any national firm. Expertise tags
make each person instantly scannable and searchable. Bios name specific districts and specific numbers.
**Weaknesses.** No pricing, no engagement structure, no clear single next step. The bench is so large
it dilutes any individual. Blocked crawlers hurt discoverability.
**What Parish should take.** The "not a career consultant, an actual former principal" frame, and
expertise tags on the founder bio. Also the discipline of naming specific numbers in a bio.
**What Parish should not copy.** Hiding the engagement model and pricing.

**Headline:** "Coaching & Consulting"
**Subheadline:** "Tailored Support to Elevate Instruction and Drive Student Success"
**Primary CTAs:** "Request a Consultation," "Explore Our Success Stories"
**Value proposition:** "Job-embedded, non-evaluative support" that builds professional trust and
collective efficacy to produce coherent instructional practice across teams.

**Design:** Navy and white corporate palette, clean sans-serif hierarchy, warm collaborative stock

**Services:** Instructional Coaching, Leadership Coaching, PLC Coaching, Data-Driven Coaching,
Curriculum Development, School Improvement Planning, Achievement Teams, Teacher Clarity Workshops,
Instructional Leadership training.

**Social proof:** Case studies teased under "Proven Results from Schools Like Yours," but the proof

## D. Patterns of the Top 10%

Five behaviors separate the high scorers from the rest of the pool.

**1. They open on the buyer's problem, not on their own name.**
K12 Coalition opens with "70% of school initiatives fail." New Leaders opens with a claim about
redefining how schools are led. The low scorers open with a category label ("K-12 Education
Consulting," "Coaching & Consulting"), which tells a superintendent nothing they did not already
know. Parish's flyer currently opens with the company name.

**2. Proof is named, numbered, and placed above the fold of the money page.**
"Promise Academy, double-digit literacy and math gains." "Graduation rates above 98 percent."
"Providence Public Schools." Vague adjectives are the mark of the bottom half. The two firms with no
on-page proof (CLS, Ventura) are the two lowest scorers on that axis despite strong service depth.

**3. The practitioner credential is the differentiator, and it is stated explicitly.**
Zeal: "not career consultants." K12 Coalition: consultants drawn from "district and state-level
roles." This is the axis on which a boutique founder-led firm beats a national brand, and the
winners say it in plain words rather than implying it through a bio.

**4. The CTA names the deliverable.**
"Request a Consultation" and "Connect with Us" outperform "Contact Us." The best of them tell the
buyer what they receive and what happens next. Not one competitor in this set publishes a price.

**5. Services are organized by district problem, not by consultant capability.**
The top sites let a buyer self-identify ("our literacy scores are flat," "we cannot keep principals")
and route to the matching offer. The weaker sites present an undifferentiated capability list.

## E. The Opening Nobody in This Set Has Taken

Three gaps are wide open, and Parish can occupy all three on day one.

**Gap 1: Published pricing.** Zero of the ten firms publish rates. Parish already has a full,
defensible five-tier pricing table sitting in the flyer. Publishing it is a genuine differentiator
that pre-qualifies every inquiry, removes the "we cannot afford them" objection before it forms, and
captures the substantial long-tail search volume around education consultant cost and rates. It also
signals confidence.

**Gap 2: A visible, singular, credentialed human.** New Leaders and CLS have no face. Zeal has a
hundred faces, which is the same as none. Ventura has a name but shows no portrait. Parish has one
high-resolution studio portrait of a doctorate-holding former principal and district leader. That is
the whole advantage, and no competitor in this set is contesting it.

**Gap 3: A named methodology.** Only Parish has one. "Our Signature 3-Phase Impact Model: Assess,
Align, Accelerate" is a proprietary, memorable, sellable framework. Not one competitor in the pool
names their process. This should be a top-level nav item and a full scroll-animated section, not a
row of three small boxes on page one of a PDF.

**Visual gap, secondary.** Nine of ten competitor sites use cold blue and white. Parish's inherited
navy, antique gold, and warm ivory palette is the most distinctive in the category by a wide margin.
It should be pushed harder on the web than it is on the flyer, not toned down.
