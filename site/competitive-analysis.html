Competitive Analysis

Where a founder-led practice wins in K-12 consulting

01

Executive summary

Parish Educational Consulting enters a market with no owned web presence at all. That is an advantage, not a deficit. There is no legacy design to unwind and no conflicting message to retire, so the launch site can be built directly against what the highest-scoring competitors are doing rather than around an inherited compromise.

Ten firms were evaluated and five were analyzed in depth. The strongest performer, New Leaders, scored 74 of 80. The weakest of the deep five, a solo practitioner site structurally identical to what Parish is about to launch, scored 54. The distance between those two numbers is almost entirely explained by three things: whether the site opens on the buyer's problem, whether proof is named and numbered on the page, and whether the human being doing the work is visible.

Three positions in this market are unoccupied. Not one of the ten firms publishes pricing. Not one of them names their methodology. And the firms that sell a single expert consistently fail to show that expert. Parish already holds all three assets: a complete five-tier pricing table, a proprietary Assess, Align, Accelerate model, and a high-resolution portrait of a doctorate-holding former principal. The recommendation of this report is to lead with all three.

04

Patterns of the top 10%

1

They open on the buyer's problem, not on their own name

K12 Coalition opens with "70% of school initiatives fail." The low scorers open with a category label such as "K-12 Education Consulting," which tells a superintendent nothing they did not already know. The current flyer opens with the company name.

2

Proof is named, numbered, and sits on the money page

Promise Academy with double-digit gains. Graduation rates above 98 percent. Providence Public Schools. The two firms with no on-page proof are the two lowest social-proof scorers despite strong service depth.

3

The practitioner credential is stated in plain words

Zeal says "not career consultants." K12 Coalition says its people came from district and state-level roles. This is the axis on which a boutique founder-led firm beats a national brand, and the winners say it outright rather than implying it through a bio.

4

The call to action names the deliverable

"Request a Consultation" and "Connect with Us" outperform "Contact Us." The strongest sites tell the buyer what they receive and what happens next.

5

Services are organized by district problem, not by consultant capability

Top sites let a buyer self-identify. "Our literacy scores are flat." "We cannot keep principals." Then they route to the matching offer. Weaker sites present an undifferentiated capability list.

Gap to close: there is no owned domain, no Google Business Profile, and no schema markup anywhere in the client's current footprint. The only indexed commercial listing prices Dr. Parish as an hourly tutor, which directly contradicts a $2,500 to $4,500 per month partnership offer. A branded site with ProfessionalService schema becomes the canonical result and resolves that conflict.

06

Three unoccupied positions

01. Publish the pricing

Zero of ten firms publish rates. Parish already has a defensible five-tier table. Publishing it pre-qualifies every inquiry, removes the affordability objection before it forms, captures long-tail cost search, and signals confidence rather than exposure.

02. Show the human being

New Leaders and Creative Leadership have no face. Zeal has a hundred, which amounts to the same thing. Advanced Collaborative has a name but shows no portrait. Parish has one high-resolution studio portrait of a doctorate-holding former principal and district leader. No competitor in this set is contesting that ground.

03. Name the methodology

Not one competitor names their process. Parish has "Assess, Align, Accelerate," a proprietary and memorable framework. It deserves a top-level navigation entry and a full scroll-animated section, not three small boxes on page one of a PDF.

07

Recommended design direction

Nine of ten competitor sites use cold blue and white. The palette inherited from the flyer, deep navy with antique gold on warm ivory, is the most distinctive in the category by a wide margin. The recommendation is to push it harder on the web than on the flyer, not to tone it down.

Palette (carried from the flyer)

  • #0A1F44 Navy, primary surface
  • #C9A646 Antique gold, accent and pricing
  • #F8F6F0 Warm ivory, page ground
  • #EAE6DB Sand, card fills
  • #5C768D Slate blue, tertiary

Typography

  • Noto Serif display and headings
  • Lato body and interface
  • Exact flyer match, both on Google Fonts
  • Serif carries authority. Every competitor uses sans-serif headings, so this alone differentiates

Structure

  • Problem-first hero, then the founder
  • Assess / Align / Accelerate as a scroll-driven section
  • Five service pillars, framed as district problems
  • Published pricing table
  • Consultation request as the single conversion goal

Motion

  • Scroll-triggered reveals on every section
  • Parallax depth on the founder portrait
  • Count-up on quantities, never on years
  • Reduced-motion respected throughout
  • Cinematic and restrained, never bouncy

What to avoid, drawn from competitor failures

  • A generic "Contact Us" button. Name the deliverable instead
  • A service page with no proof on it, as on Creative Leadership
  • Personal branding with no portrait, as on Advanced Collaborative
  • Burying the core offer under product navigation, as on K12 Coalition
  • Cold corporate blue, which nine of ten competitors already own
  • Invented statistics. Where a number cannot be sourced, use honest qualitative phrasing
